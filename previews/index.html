Why Gunnersbury moves need local knowledge

The London Moving House Checklist: 8 Weeks to Moving Day

Get a Free Quote

Moving house anywhere is stressful. Moving house in London throws in controlled parking zones, managed buildings with lift bookings, congestion and ULEZ rules, and narrow Victorian streets where a lorry can only just squeeze through. After years of moving homes across the capital from our base in Chiswick, we've put together the checklist we wish every customer had from day one.

Work through it week by week and moving day becomes something you've planned rather than something that happens to you.

8 weeks before: set the foundations

Book your removal survey. Good removal companies get booked up, especially at month-end, on Fridays and in the summer. A proper survey, in person or by video, means you get an accurate, fixed quote rather than a guess. At K1 every quote is tailored to your home after a free survey. Book a survey

Decide what's coming with you. Go room by room and sort everything into keep, sell, donate or dump. Every item you don't move saves packing time and space on the vehicle. London's charity shops, the British Heart Foundation furniture collection service and local reuse groups make decluttering easier than ever.

Start a moving folder. Keep your completion or tenancy dates, the removal quote, solicitor or agent contacts, building rules and all your reference numbers in one place, paper or digital.

Check your new building's rules. If you're moving into or out of a managed block, ask the concierge or managing agent about: - permitted move days and times - lift booking and lift protection - loading bay access - whether they need an insurance certificate or a risk assessment from your removal company

Think about storage. If your dates don't line up, ask about short-term storage now rather than in the last week. Removals and storage

6 weeks before: book the big things

Confirm your removal date as soon as exchange looks likely. Many movers will hold a provisional date.

Decide on packing. Will you pack yourself, have the professionals pack everything, or just pay for fragile packing? For large homes, full packing the day before the move saves a huge amount of stress. Packing services

4 weeks before: London-specific admin

Arrange the parking suspension. This is the step Londoners forget most often. If there's no guaranteed legal space for a removal lorry outside either property, you'll need a parking bay suspension from the council. Most London councils want at least 10 working days' notice, and some want more. A good removal company will apply for you. Our guide to parking suspensions in Hounslow, Ealing and Hammersmith & Fulham

Check ULEZ and the Congestion Charge. The Ultra Low Emission Zone covers all London boroughs. If you're hiring a van yourself, check it's compliant. Moving in or out of central London on a weekday? The Congestion Charge may apply too.

Redirect your post. Royal Mail's redirection service takes a few days to set up, so do it now.

Update your details. Make a list of everyone who needs your new address: bank, employer, HMRC, DVLA (driving licence and V5C), GP, dentist, insurance providers, pensions, subscriptions, TV Licensing and the electoral roll.

Council tax. Tell both your old and new London councils when you're moving, so you're billed correctly.

2 weeks before: pack and prepare

Start packing the rooms you use least: lofts, guest rooms, books, out-of-season clothes and the garage. Label every box with the room and a short description. Our packing tips for large homes

Plan for children and pets. Moving day is much easier if young children and pets are with friends or family, or in day care.

1 week before: final checks

  • Use up the food in your freezer.
  • Defrost the fridge and freezer 24–48 hours before the move.
  • Return keys, library books and borrowed items.
  • Sort out valuables: jewellery, passports, documents, chargers and medicines should travel with you, not in the lorry.
  • Pack an essentials box (see below).
  • Measure large furniture and check it fits through the new doors, stairs and lift.

The day before

  • If you've booked packing, the packers will usually arrive now.
  • Take apart anything you've agreed to dismantle yourself, and keep the screws in labelled bags taped to the furniture.
  • Charge your phone and keep it with you.
  • Make sure your removal company knows exactly where the parking suspension is and has the building contact.

Moving day

  • Be ready for the crew's arrival time, and walk the supervisor through the house.
  • Point out anything that's not going.
  • Take meter readings and photos at the old property.
  • Do a final check of every room, cupboard, loft, shed and garden.
  • At the new home, stand at the door and direct each item to the right room. Labels make this quick.
  • Check that the beds are rebuilt before the crew leaves, so you have somewhere to sleep.

The first week in your new home

  • Register with a GP and dentist.
  • Check your council tax and parking permit at the new address. Many London CPZs need you to apply for a resident permit straight away.

Parking Suspensions for Removals in Hounslow, Ealing and Hammersmith & Fulham

Get a Free Quote

Ask any London removal crew what ruins a moving day and you'll hear the same answer: nowhere to park. A removal lorry parked two streets away can add hours of carrying to your move, and parking on a yellow line or in a resident bay without permission can mean a penalty charge notice or even a tow.

The answer is a parking bay suspension, where the council reserves the bays outside your property for your removal. This guide explains how it works in the three West London boroughs we move people around most: Hounslow, Ealing, and Hammersmith & Fulham.

Council rules, fees and online forms change, so always check the current details on your council's website before applying. Or let your removal company handle it for you, as we do for K1 customers.

What is a parking suspension?

A parking suspension temporarily takes one or more parking bays out of normal use, usually for a day or a few hours, so a specific vehicle can use them. The council (or its contractor) puts up suspension signs in advance, warning other drivers not to park there.

You may also hear about: - Dispensations: permission for a vehicle to wait on a yellow line or in a restricted area, where loading would normally be banned. Used where there are no bays to suspend. - Loading exemptions: on many single yellow lines, loading and unloading is already allowed for a limited time. A full house move usually takes longer than the limit allows, though.

Do you actually need one?

You probably need a suspension if: - your street is in a controlled parking zone (CPZ) with residents' or pay-and-display bays - there's no off-street parking or driveway big enough for a removal lorry - your street is narrow and double-parking would block traffic - you're moving into or out of a flat above shops on a busy high street - your building's loading bay is too small for a removal vehicle

You probably don't if you have a large driveway, or you're on a quiet uncontrolled street with plenty of space (but still check on the morning).

Hounslow (Chiswick, Brentford, Isleworth, Hounslow, Feltham)

The London Borough of Hounslow covers more than people think: Chiswick W4, Brentford TW8, Isleworth TW7, Hounslow TW3/TW4/TW5 and Feltham. Many streets near stations and town centres are CPZs.

How it typically works: - Apply through Hounslow Council's parking suspension service on its website. - Give the address, the dates and times, the number of bays and the vehicle details. - Notice: councils normally ask for at least 10 working days. In practice, apply two to three weeks ahead, especially for month-end moves. - Costs: usually an admin or application fee plus a charge per bay per day. Check current rates. - Signs are put up in advance. Take a photo of them as evidence.

Local tips: - Chiswick: the roads off Chiswick High Road and around Turnham Green are heavily parked, so suspend at least two bays for a lorry. - Brentford: on matchdays at the Gtech Community Stadium, extra restrictions may apply nearby. - Isleworth: in Old Isleworth's narrow streets, a smaller vehicle plus a suspension can work better than a big lorry.

Ealing (Ealing, West Ealing, Hanwell, Acton, Southall)

Ealing Council covers W5, W13, W7, W3 (Acton) and further west. There are lots of CPZs around Ealing Broadway, West Ealing, Acton town centre and the stations.

How it typically works: - Apply online through Ealing Council's parking suspension pages. - You'll be asked for the location, the dates, the number of bays and the reason (house removal). - Notice: again, plan on at least 10 working days. Two to three weeks is safer. - Costs: usually an application fee plus a charge per bay per day. - If you're moving out of one Ealing address and into another, you may need two suspensions.

Local tips: - Low-traffic neighbourhoods: some Ealing streets have filters, so check the lorry's route to your door, not just the parking. - Large family homes around Ealing Common and Pitshanger often need two vehicles, which means more bays. - New-build blocks near the station may have their own loading bay rules on top of council rules.

Hammersmith & Fulham (Hammersmith, Fulham, Shepherd's Bush, White City)

H&F has some of the most tightly controlled streets in West London. W6, SW6, W12 and W14 are almost entirely CPZs, and many streets are residents-only.

How it typically works: - Apply through H&F Council's parking suspension service. - Supply the address, the dates and times, the bays needed and the vehicle details. - Notice: budget for at least 10 working days. Apply as soon as your date is confirmed. - Costs: usually an application or admin fee plus a per-bay daily charge. - Dispensations may be needed where there are only yellow lines.

Local tips: - Hammersmith Bridge is closed to motor traffic, so south-of-river moves need a different route. That doesn't affect your suspension, but it does affect timing. - Fulham's terraced streets are narrow and very full, so a suspension is almost always essential. - White City's new developments often require a booked loading bay through the building as well.

How many bays do you need?

As a rough guide: - Large van: 1–2 bays - 7.5-tonne removal lorry: 2–3 bays - Two vehicles, or a lorry plus a van: 3–4 bays

Your removal company should tell you exactly what you need after the survey.

Common mistakes

  1. Applying too late. The most common mistake by far. If you miss the notice period, the council may refuse your application.
  2. Too few bays. A lorry that doesn't fit is as bad as no suspension.
  3. Wrong times. Allow for an early start and an overrun.
  4. Forgetting the other end. You might need suspensions at both properties, in two different boroughs.
  5. Not checking the signs. Make sure they've gone up and are correct a few days before.
  6. Cars still parked. If someone parks in a suspended bay, report it to the council's enforcement line straight away. Councils can ticket or relocate the vehicle.

What happens on the day?

Your crew parks in the suspended bays, displaying the permit or reference if needed. If a car is in the way, the crew or you contact the council's parking enforcement. A good crew arrives early to secure the space.

Most London councils ask for at least 10 working days. We recommend applying two to three weeks ahead.
